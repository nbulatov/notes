## To omit something 

>Omit is a verb that means to not include something either by mistake or intentionally

Examples:
- A list of new employees inadvertenly *omitted* her name
- The Prince's tour conveniently *omitted* the most deprived areas of the city
- The articles author made untrue statements and *omitted* key information

Examples:

- The kids *jubled up* the cards during the game
- I always *jumble up* the twins' name

## A derivation 

> Derivation is a noun that means the origin of source of something; in language, it refers to the way a new word is formed from an existing word or root, often by adding prefixes or suffixes

Examples:
- The *derivation* of the word "biology" comes from Greek words for "life" and "study."
- She explained the *derivation* of this mathematical formula step by step
- In linguistics class, we studied the *derivation* of adjective from nouns, like "child" becoming "childlish"

## Gradually /'grædʒ.u.ə.li/

> **Gradually** is an adverb that means slowly over a period of time; happening in small steps or degrees rather than suddenly

Examples:
- The weather is *gradually* getting warmer as spring arrives
- She *gradually* learned to speak French after living in Paris for a year
- His eyesight has been *gradually* getting worse since he turned sixty

## Perversion /pə'vɜ:.ʃən/

> **Perversion** is a noun that means a change or distortion of something from its original or natural state, especially in a way that is unnatural, harmful, or morally wrong; it can refer to behaviour, ideas, or the use of something

Examples:
- His kindness was a *perversion* of the truth, as he was actually manipulating people
- The new law was as a *perversion* of justice by its opponents
- Some people consider that practice to be a *perversion* of the original religious teaching
